5.88, log2 4.57 / 5.56, log10 3.62 / 3.52, log1p 3.68 / 3.85. Counts (job keke:libm-coverage-1107-percall, FMA builds):
log 190 vs 66, log2 195 vs 66, log10 204 vs 106, log1p 243 vs 85; SSE2 builds in keke:libm-coverage-1107-percall-sse2
(log 225.6 vs 81 in LIBM-RETHINK-2). Tier today: the fast64 log tier is an accurate phase (~2^-70) on every call

Step 1 (kernel story part, ~30 calls): find the serialising step. `validation/wl_trace.sh` one call of log (both builds),
and a latency/throughput pair with the LIBM-RETHINK-2 harness (`handoff/libm-rethink/proto/proto_time.c`,
`run_time.sh`) on a booked Ivy Bridge: candidates an MXCSR write, a division chain, a store-forward stall through
`black_box`. Fix it alone first if it is the envelope (counts + booked timing), commit.

Step 2: the quick phase. METHOD (literature, not the reference): Tang's table-driven log [Tang, ACM TOMS 16(4) 1990]
with a 128-entry table of (1/c_j rounded, log c_j as double + low part) generated by `cargo xtask tables`, r = x·(1/c_j)
− 1 exact by FMA or by choosing 1/c_j with few significant bits (Gal's accurate-table idea [Gal & Bachelis, TOMS 17(1)
1991] so no low part is needed for r on SSE2); a minimax polynomial of degree 5-6 from a rigorous fpminimax-style fit
(Brisebarre-Chevillard, "Efficient polynomial L∞ approximations", ARITH 2007), evaluated in Estrin's scheme for
latency; quick bound ~2^-62..-65; Ziv test `near` (one-sided) with the constant from that bound [Ziv 1991; CRlibm report
2006, rounding test theorem]; tier 2 = today's fast64 log (unchanged proofs), tier 3 = mp. log2/log10 use the same
table with pre-scaled constants (D-10.13 log-tables work in saturation §16 already gives the scaled tables); log1p uses
the quick log on 1+x formed exactly (Fast2Sum) with |x| < 2^-5 by its own short polynomial. Builds: SSE2 (Ivy Bridge),
VEX, FMA through D-10.50's load-time dispatch (method g); bit-identical results.
Split if large: kernel + proof + tests (this story), wiring per function (`-WIRE` brief) when the files are large
(`fast64.rs` > 40 KB: grep -n, read by range).
